Chapter 2


	I start to shake off the “zone out”, which usually happens when I overthink, realizing I’m right where I should be. Jerry's is a cool-looking place from the outside. Inviting, with chairs and tables set up along a wide sidewalk at the corner of a quieter part of our main road. Walking in, I can’t help but get nostalgic vibez from this clean, up-to-code, updated 1970’s pizzeria. Maybe it's Sal’s pizzeria from do the right thing. Maybe that's the look they were going for or maybe that's what I want it to be. I head to the counter head on a swivel when a family in the back corner catches my eye; here whenever I am……. The chances of us being here at the exact time are slim, especially when my trips to Jerry's are random with no rhyme or reason. So I guess they are regulars…..or plants, compliments of the owner, basking in synthetic light while giving the place the feeling of family. Something Jerry’s couldn't achieve with decor, instead enlisting the help of good-hearted people in bringing the place together. More so than any generic piece of art or gaudy talking piece could ever do. The restaurant industry’s version of hot  social girls at a club? Kind of, except this family is a tad overweight–a testament to the food–wholesome and aren't trying to get you to buy a new bottle. They do share one thing in common though—with the girls who are on and setting the scene—they won't screw you.

 	I make my way to the counter taking note of whose back there and their roles. Closer to the pizza showcase, two young ladies maybe in their early twenties. One’s finishing bagging an order and the other ringing up the customer ahead of me. They look alike, adding credence to my hypothesis; sisters……………………………… Sherlock Holmes eat your heart out.  
Holding in their agitation for one another, knowing their mother, who happens to be standing close behind, scolds them for having it out with each other in front of customers. A problem and a pattern I suppose, their mother expressing this issue with her plants……I mean lovable “customers”, the last time I was here. Something about telling her kids to love one another, especially now that one is a mother and the other a beloved aunt. I’m guessing

	Anyway, the tones shifted, since the last time I was here, Mom or I guess Grandmom depending on who you ask, eyes laser-focused on the two of them. She’s given up on pumping them with granola lines like “you should really love one another……you’re all ya got”, instead appealing to their practical side; Don’t be at each other's throats, in front of customers because it's bad for business. The sisters seem similar enough, in mannerisms, which makes their disdain for one another  completely understandable. Living and working with your sister, as each tries to stand out and establish their own identity, must be tough. They are both easy on the eyes and quick to come up with a smile when a customer approaches. I’m really intrigued by both of them, but the frustrated, optimistic yet practical mother is the main character of this story.
