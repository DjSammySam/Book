She learned through looking and asking but never got any hands-on practice. She is very skilled in dealing with the front end, just like her mother, but only really puts her hand on a pot or pan if the business is in dire need. It’s clear her grandparents skill in the kitchen never rub off on mary.  Mary would tell you the root of this issue is not enough practical experience. You can only learn so much watching from the dining room, especially when you have one eye on the door–calling out when customers come in.

David and Bonnie’s grandparents, who died before either were born, were super-tough on Jimmy and Sylvana,  bordering on bullying. Being in a strange country where the “strong survive” seeps into one’s pores and personality especially when language is a barrier.

In turn, giving their son God-like confidence that wasn’t rooted in preparation or being earnest. Often causing Bonnie and David to butt heads,  where david was often the aggressor–particularly as they got older and started working in the restaurant side by side.  Bonnie naturally gravitated to the front end. She’s a knockout, curvy–she thinks she's fat….she’s wrong…… with long brown hair that’s often in a bun. Bonnie’s looks could make any customer uneasy, but that would soon dissipate after a few minutes of conversation as her happy face, subtle smile, and playful eyebrow raise coupled with the easiness of her voice helps strangers overcome the initial sticker shock. Bonnie’s presence in the front end gave David a path to the kitchen, one that he claimed and never let go of.  Never letting Bonnie and by extension Mary in the kitchen.

But her appeasement, letting David monopolize the kitchen was more listening to her parents' silence than backing down from David's “man-spread”. She adored them but more importantly, she respected their business acumen. She followed them not out of blind love–because thats never been Bonnie’s style— but because she saw through other avenues their smarts and business savvy. The restaurant is their main business venture–and as the restaurant went so did they–but they diversified their investments,  a little here and there on the block. Crack? Mafia ties? Lol no. Investing in dull products like a local grocer and a hardware store in return for shares of the profit. Silent partners whose wisdom was sought often.  No contract, no accounting just one another’s words. Something Mary and David couldn’t believe whenever her grandparents tell the story. How could they just trust random people? Both with the beliefs that people aren’t bad but they aren’t exactly immaculate; At the end of the day, they are human. Maybe it wasn’t that mary’s grandparents trusted the individual grocer or hardware store owner but they trusted people and their community….because betting against the people of this community is always a losing one. 

	So with this–at least through Bonnie’s eyes–maybe marys grandparents, Jimmy and Sylvana,  saw something in David that Bonnie didn’t. They have more business experience than she did.  She couldn’t fathom, that their love for their only son could ever cloud their judgment–a testament to the respect and patience she has for her parents–so when the benefit of the doubt arose, it always fell at her parents' feet. Whenever the feeling of getting the short end of the stick crept into her mind, Bonnie grounded herself and rationally came to the conclusion…maybe she was biased. Bonnie is humble but competitive—at least through her eyes— maybe her competitive nature stops her from seeing the talent in others. She’s self-aware and introspective so she doesn’t make a stink about her brother's role in the business and lets time render the final settlement on her parents' bet. 

	One the other hand her daughter–Mary a spunky young lady with her mother's eyes and brown hair that curls if isn’t straighten daily– was not about that shit. Mary hated how rude Dave was to her mother. More importantly, he fucking sucked. He couldn’t cook for the life of him. In her tirades, Mary wouldn’t even bring up how Friday and Saturday nights he was out with his friends drinking, because honestly, that was the best thing he ever did. At least he wasn’t cooking during the busiest nights of the week. On those nights, the professionals got to run their kitchen and they did a damn good job. Food was bomb, even getting compliments from marys school friends Monday morning. Mary was vocal about what she saw and heard, quick to tell her uncle that he wasn’t any good, something he laughed off, smug and dismissive, assuming that she must have overheard this from her mother—which whether said or unsaid drove a deeper rivet in his relationship with his sister. 

	Dave and Bonnie’s relationship was complicated by the fact they didn’t communicate all that well—especially as they got older.  Bonnie gave Dave the benefit of the doubt, but he doesn’t know that. Dave viewed the kitchen as his domicile one where the heat was hot and chatter only added unnecessary pressure. He felt this way about a lot of things, especially when he was in school. It didn’t help that Bonnie would get in the face and be super tough when he was doing his homework. Bonnie is trying to rule the world and doesn’t need her family's name sullied because little old Dave can’t do his times' table. Bonnie made it to clear that he will become a member of the unsullied if he didn’t fix this real quick.  Bonnie was spoken to about this and changed her ways and style…..Limiting her berating and constant pressuring of Dave to when her parents weren’t home, especially early on in grade school. The only place Dave would find peace was the kitchen of the restaurant–because Bonnie wasn’t allowed in–and the kitchen of their home–a place Bonnie never lingered as she was always watching her figure. 
Mary wasn’t mean per say–like her mother–just bad at delivery; ironically so was the pizzeria. She was fair and as quick as she was to dish out criticism, she gave praise where praise was due; often telling her grandparents about all the compliments they got on weekend service. They were flattered but just thought it was the neighborhood kids being nice. Grandparents clearly didn’t go to elementary school in America because kids are really mean–something her uncle knew all too well–even before the days of finstas and whatnot.
Mary–whose passion was strong but hadn’t been softened by living long enough to make her own mistakes–also couldn’t stand indifference. Her favorite bible quote–as religion was a big part of their lives–a product of her mother's “gentle push”--wasn’t the first that came to mind.

	Dave and Ivan have a unique friendship that differs based on the prism you look through. Ivan always had a soft spot for Dave. Unaware as to why and oblivious in the moment, he always looked out for Dave. Ivan is a few years older than Dave so they never had any classes together. However, Ivan made it a point to say what's up to Dave in the hallway and tell the snot-nose kid to knock it off whenever Dave was on the receiving end of a hard time. He also shot the shit with him from time to time when their paths crossed… walking home late, each for different reasons. At the behest of his sister and parents, Dave stayed after for extra help in math while Ivan would hang out aimlessly in an empty gym throwing fastballs at a soccer net. Walking around the building hoping to ask a teacher a question that he already knew the answer to til the janitor, eventually, caught him and kicked him out. Ivan did this for other kids, sure, but everyone noticed he did it a little more for Dave. They would talk baseball and math on the walk home, each a little more knowledgable in the topic than the other thought. Dave was apprehensive as to why he took a liking to him but ivan would subtly reassure him that it's nice to have someone to walk home with. Cool by Dave, as now he felt comfortable taking the short route home, the one he normally avoided. The route that passed by the library where his sister would always do homework with her friend Bernadette and the corner store where all the bigger kids in his grade would post up drinking the soda-pop and cracking jokes at passer-byers. 

	The corner store that ivan cousin Lars runs.. Lars comes from a good family, and is about 8 years older then Ivan, but runs astray. He hasn’t graduated to actual criminal activity yet— only doing dumb stuff like breaking street-level windows with his boots, getting into fights, and prank calling the police station—but there’s a chance that these are precursors to something bigger. His family is happy he's holding down a job but little do they know his commitment to selling high-sodium beef jerky is just a guise for selling loosies to kids while hitting on whatever girls walkthrough. Ivan takes heed of the warnings from his parents about Lars keeping interactions to a minimum. Friendly enough not to participate in some ill-schemed spiteful setup but smart and confident to come up with an excuse when Lars needs him to “help out real quick”.
